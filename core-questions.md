*Why should this exist?*
To democratize the process of urban planning, ensuring fairer outcomes for all.

*Describe one specific person who would use this. How do you know?*
The local advocate who wishes to improve their neighborhood, but lacks the resources to show off their vision. They have identified a core problem at a particular part of where they live, and now they wish to present this idea to other advocates and to City Council.
I know this because I have spoken to both advocates and city council members. The city council members tell me that the most valuable thing an advocate can do is to bring a fully-fleshed idea to them rather than just a feeling. The advocates tell me the difficulty of such a thing.

*Does it need to be informed by domain research & expertise?*
Most likely; urban planning is more an art dictated by our own rules rather than a science with one best approach. However, an expert opinion or knowledge (perhaps taken from a comprehensive planning document) is best used when considering what to create.
For example, a neighborhood comprehensive plan might state the need to assist a low-income community without incurring gentrification; it is in this example that to build housing is to build affordable, rather than luxury housing.

*Describe the user's POV.*
The user is given a map with the ability to select certain portions and describe what should be improved. The user should be specific and should focus on a specific area. Even if the broader goal is to make sweeping changes, they must specifically specify how it will change their area of interest.
It should feel natural and easy to select certain parts of the urban environment -- simply clicking on an area should highlight the entirety of the object selected.

*What is the core irreplaceable feature? What is the role of Gen AI?*
The ability to instantly generate imagery of reimagined urban fabric is the core feature.
The role of generative AI is to generate this imagery, using additional context from the user, the surrounding environment, and any details from the city's documents on how the area is to develop long-term.

*List the concerns this project raises. Who could be harmed?*
Urban planning as a field aims to improve the urban standard of living for all while minimizing harm done to the most vulnerable. Historically, many projects have negative impacts that effect marginalized communities to this day: highways that divide neighborhoods, slum clearance measures, underfunded public housing are but a few of the challenges posed. It will be imperative to minimize risk done to communities when forming new projects -- eminant domain and redevelopment must be a scalpel, not a hammer.

*What are you setting aside for now?*
The gamified portions will be set aside for now. In the future, I'd like to have a points system that encourage positive collaboration over unilateral change, and design contests encouraged by local governments to democratize the design process.

*How do you know it's working?*
I know it will be working when it starts to feel natural in a similar sense to that of how most advocates explore their digital versions of their neighborhood: they look at satellite maps, use Google Street View imagery, and annotate all over it.
